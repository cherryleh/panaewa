Keaʻahuli ʻO Panaʻewa

Poly-Forestry and Weather Dashboard

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), in partnership with the Keaukaha-Panaʻewa Farmers Association (KPFA) and other collaborators, has launched a pilot project to co-produce an agroforestry weather dashboard for the Keaukaha and Panaʻewa communities on the Island of Hawaiʻi. This initiative integrates traditional agroforestry knowledge with modern climate science and data, with the goal of enhancing local resilience to climate change.

The project also supports the establishment of a 2-acre food forest within the Panaʻewa Hawaiian Homestead to:

  • Build farmer and community knowledge about climate change effects
  • Foster community engagement and agro-resilience
  • Combine traditional Hawaiian agroforestry practices with NOAA’s climate data and expertise

Our Mission

Our mission is to co-produce a community-driven agricultural and weather dashboard that brings together local weather data, traditional agroforestry knowledge, and modern climate science. Through this effort, the Keaʻahuli ʻO Panaʻewa project seeks to develop a resilient poly-forestry food system adapted to Hawaiʻi’s unique environmental conditions—strengthening food security, supporting agricultural education, and fostering a deeper connection between people, place, and climate.
